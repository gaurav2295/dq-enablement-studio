## Goal

Read a skeptical or hostile room correctly, open with the line that actually lands for that
persona, and hold your ground on a challenged number without ever conceding one you can't trace.

## When to use

- Before opening the file, when you already know the persona and can guess the room's temperature
- The moment a figure gets challenged: "can I put this in the budget?"
- The moment credibility gets tested directly: "are these numbers even real?"
- Spotting (or training someone to spot) a script that leaks credibility mid-session

## Prerequisites

- Know the four altitudes and the persona-to-outcome mapping — see
  [[con-audience-altitude|Audience Altitude]]
- Know the say/never-say table cold — see
  [[std-client-vocabulary|Client Vocabulary — Say/Never-Say]]

## Offensive vs. defensive language

A deliberate mix, not an either/or — which register to use depends on where you are in the
conversation:

- **Offensive (lead with it):** the outcome, and the value potential it points at. This is how you
  open and how you re-open after an objection — put the size of the opportunity in front of the
  client before they've asked for it.
- **Defensive (hold ground on it):** *[[gls-indicative|indicative]]* and *traceability*. This is
  how you answer a challenge — never by defending the number itself, only by showing what it's
  made of and changing it in front of them.

**Phrase the outcome offensively, not the problem.** The manufacturing case is the clearest
example: the offensive form is *"reduce unplanned downtime,"* not *"unplanned downtime."* Lead
with the outcome you're moving the client toward, not the problem you found. The phrasing
discipline holds regardless of whether that particular outcome is in the published catalogue —
see [[con-outcome-hierarchy-l1-l5]] for its status.

## Persona × room temperature

Pick a persona and a room temperature before you open. **All three personas have a worked cold
opening line** — they are in
[[con-audience-altitude|Audience Altitude]] alongside the outcomes, measures and fears that go
with them. What varies below is the *room temperature* layer on top of that line: only the CFO is
scripted warm-versus-skeptical. The other two are honestly marked planned, not faked — do not
improvise a false confidence you don't have yet.

### CFO — implemented

- **Warm room** (receptive, early in a business-case cycle): open offensively — lead with the
  outcome and the value potential. *"This is the size of the case your data is already making —
  evidenced rule by rule."*
- **Skeptical room** (has been burned by a soft number before, or mid business-case scrutiny):
  open defensively — lead with indicative and traceability before the value potential lands. Do
  not let the value potential be the first sentence they hear.

### COO — planned

Use the COO cold opening in [[con-audience-altitude|Audience Altitude]]
(*"Here's what breaks operationally when this data goes uncorrected — and exactly where it
breaks."*), then stay defensive. The **warm/skeptical variants are not yet scripted** — pending
live-session experience. Persona mapping (operational-consequence framing, inventory/downtime
outcomes) is in the same unit.

### CDO / CIO / CTO — planned

Use the data-leadership cold opening in [[con-audience-altitude|Audience Altitude]]
(*"This gives your data programme the funding case and the mandate it doesn't have today —
traceable from the board's language down to the rule that proves it."*), then stay defensive. The
**warm/skeptical variants are not yet scripted** — pending live-session experience. Persona
mapping (mandate/funding framing, L2→L5 traceability) is in the same unit.

## Handling "so can I put this in the budget?"

The single most likely misreading, and the one that costs you credibility if you fudge it. Answer
plainly and immediately:

> **No — and that's deliberate.** This is the size of the case, not money we're promising. It's the
> value your data is standing in front of: the opportunity you could build a business case
> against, evidenced rule by rule. What you'd take to the budget is the case we build *from* it —
> with your own figures, your own rates, and the evidence behind each one.

Then move straight to proof rather than defending the number:

1. Open Value Studio and show the base, the rate, and where the rate came from.
2. Change a rate in front of them and let the total move — this converts a claimed figure into a
   shared model faster than any explanation.
3. Point at the chain: figure → assumption → driver → rule that will run on their data.

**Never** say "conservative", "guaranteed", "you'll save", or attach a payback period to the value
potential. If pushed for a bankable number, the honest answer is that it comes *after* the
engagement, from measured results — that's precisely what the blueprint delivers.

## Handling "are these numbers even real?"

The honest answer, in order — never defend a number you cannot trace; changing it in front of the
client is always the stronger move:

1. **They are indicative, and the sheet says so** — every figure is labelled and carries its
   basis.
2. **Open Value Studio.** Show the base (their own anchor figure), the rate, and where the rate
   came from. Change it in front of them and let the total move.
3. **Show the chain.** Every figure traces to a business rule that will run against their data.
4. **State the limit plainly:** nothing here is measured yet. The engagement replaces every
   assumption with evidence, and overlapping value categories are never double-counted in the
   delivered blueprint.

## Spot the credibility leak — drill

A short mock exchange contains one line that breaks a say/never-say rule. The drill: read the
transcript, identify the line that leaks, and name which rule from
[[std-client-vocabulary|Client Vocabulary — Say/Never-Say]] it breaks. Run this drill on your own
scripts before a live session, not just on prepared examples — a leak is easiest to hear in
someone else's words and easiest to miss in your own.

## Verification

- You can answer "can I put this in the budget?" without pausing, using the model pitch above as
  the shape of the answer, not a script to recite word for word.
- You can name, for your assigned persona, which room-temperature opening you'd use before you've
  opened the file.
- Given a mock transcript, you can point at the exact line that leaks and name the broken rule.

## Common pitfalls

- Opening offensively (leading with value potential) into a skeptical CFO room — it reads as
  overselling a number that hasn't earned trust yet. Lead defensively instead.
- Defending a challenged number instead of showing its working — the strong move is always to open
  Value Studio and change the rate live, not to argue the figure is right.
- Improvising a scripted opening line for COO or CDO/CIO/CTO personas before they're calibrated —
  those are honestly marked planned; use the persona mapping in
  [[con-audience-altitude|Audience Altitude]] and stay defensive until a live session earns the
  line.
- Using a banned word (see [[std-client-vocabulary|Client Vocabulary — Say/Never-Say]]) while under
  pressure from a challenge — this is exactly the moment a credibility leak happens.
