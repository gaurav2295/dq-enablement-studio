## Goal

Facilitate a #bob-dq Business Outcomes Canvas session — the client picks a business outcome, the
instrument walks it down to the business data drivers and rules that evidence it, and the session
ends with an exported working file that carries the client's priority straight into delivery.

This is a **concepts-first** engagement: know the inversion (down to sell, up to prove) before you
touch the tool. The instrument is the proof of the concepts, not a substitute for them.

## When to use

- Running the Business Outcomes Canvas live in front of a client (any of the three plays below)
- Rehearsing before a first live session
- Onboarding a new facilitator (AE, presales, architect, delivery partner)

## Prerequisites

- Know the four altitudes and who each is pitched at — see
  [[con-audience-altitude|Audience Altitude]]
- Know the say/never-say table cold — see [[std-client-vocabulary|Client Vocabulary — Say/Never-Say]]
- Know which of the three plays you're in **before you open the file** — the tier boundary does
  most of the discipline for you

## The three plays — pick one before opening the file

| | Play 1 · The executive showcase | Play 2 · Building trust | Play 3 · Internal, to project start |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facilitator | Presales, AE, delivery partner | Presales, AE, architect, principal, delivery partner | Presales, delivery partner, C5/C6 |
| Audience | IT & Business Executives, Global Governance Director, Project Director | Global DQ Lead, Data Workstream Lead, Data Director, Data Transformation & Governance Lead, SAP Data Director | Internal Syniti |
| The story | What business outcomes are driven by data — the Syniti difference | The value proposition and USP — what can't my SI or in-house give me? | Initiation → proposal → commercial → project start, ASAP |
| The arc | Intro → Canvas → Solution Proposal. **Stop.** | Canvas → Rule Explorer (pick → propose → **Promote**, narrated) → Solution Proposal → present "Solution Proposal — Detailed" full-screen | Engagement end-to-end: intake → sizing → documents → the dq-studio handover tab. Save working file **is** the handover act. |
| Exit state | A room that wants the next conversation — no commercials appeared, the tier made sure | A scoped package with named evidence; the commercial conversation carried by a watermarked document | A travelling working file dq-studio can import; documents Finance/Legal can review |
| Never on screen | Investment, rates, hours, the internal workbench, Rule Explorer detail | The internal workbench tabs; a euro entering the headline without a narrated Promote | *(internal — nothing gated, speed is the point)* |

## Steps — the demo script

A model session, start to Save working file. Client-tier modes, in journey order.

### 1. Open on Introduction

Don't explain the tool — say the inversion out loud first: *"Most data work starts with rules and
hopes the value shows up later. This starts with the outcome you already care about, and walks
down to the evidence for it — then back up into a prioritised plan."* Move to Canvas once the
client is nodding, not before.

Introduction's own framing is the **blueprint story** — one building, told top-down, the same
direction as "down to sell." Play it before a single figure appears:

| Level | Product term | What you point at |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Business outcome | **The finished building** — the ambition itself: a renovated landmark, a hotel, a tower. The kind of goal the client already has in mind. |
| 2 | Business data drivers | **The structure** — pull the finish away and expose what makes the building *the building*: the inner truss, the load-bearing members. |
| 3 | Business rules | **The foundation — and the lifeblood.** The blocks it all rests on, *and* the services running up through every floor: water, power, data. Which is why one defect is felt in more than one place. |

### 2. Land in Canvas at the outcome level

Point at two or three outcome cards and ask which one is live for them right now — don't pick for
them. Drag their answer to the top; say out loud that the order they just gave you travels into
the export as their stated priority.

### 3. Walk one outcome down

Click through outcome → measure → business data driver → business rule on the outcome they chose.
At each level, say what that level is *for*, not what it's called — use the altitude table in
[[con-audience-altitude|Audience Altitude]] as your script. Use the explainer island as a
teleprompter: click the card, paraphrase what appears out loud.

### 4. Show a "moves" connection

Pick a business data driver and show the other outcomes it also touches — this is where the
client sees that one fix pays into more than one of their numbers.

### 5. Open Value Studio only when a number gets challenged

Not before. Show the base, the rate, and where the rate came from. Change a rate in front of them
and let the total move — this is the strongest tool in the session; don't spend it early. See
[[prc-handle-the-cold-room|Handle the Cold Room]] for the full challenge-handling sequence.

### 6. Open Rule Explorer when the room asks "is this real"

At the rule level — it's the proof layer. If rules get picked, open the Summary and narrate the
proposed band: greyed, not counted, one Promote away. Promote in front of them or not at all — the
click is part of the theatre, because it is the honesty made visible.

### 7. End the client arc in Solution Proposal

Once the outcome set and priority order feel settled — the journey, the phases, time to value.
Play 1 stops here. In Play 2, the commercial conversation continues as a document, presented
full-screen; the internal workbench tabs are never on a client's screen.

### 8. Close by naming the author field and saving

Say, out loud, what Save working file produces: the internal specification that drives delivery,
not a client leave-behind — the finished blueprint is what they receive later. Complete the author
field before you save.

## The eight-module map (what each proves)

One practice loop per module: attempt, immediate feedback, try again — difficulty comes from
realism, not from a score.

| Module | Name | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| M0 | The Idea | You can say what a business outcome *is*, and the inversion it depends on, in your own words, in about a minute. |
| M1 | The Chain | You know the three client-facing levels of the chain — outcome, driver, rule — and where the money actually lives, with real examples. |
| M2 | The Words | You can translate between the room's language and the delivery team's language without saying either out loud in the wrong place. |
| M3 | The Instrument | You know the six modes and the two tiers as a journey, what happens in each, and what to say while you're in it. |
| M4 | The Number | You can hold your ground on "can I put this in the budget?" and "are these numbers real?" without conceding a number you can't trace. |
| M5 | To Implementation | You know what happens after the canvas closes — the path from a working file to a delivered blueprint — and who cares at each altitude. |
| M6 | The Cold Room | You can read a persona and a room temperature and open with the line that lands, and you can hear a credibility leak. |
| M7 | The Plays | You know which of the three engagement plays you are in before you open the file, and what must never appear on screen during it. |

## Operating checklist — in-room essentials

- Nothing is stored in the page — save the working file before closing the tab.
- The explainer island is your teleprompter — click any card, paraphrase what appears out loud.
- Drag outcome cards to capture the client's priority order live — it travels into the export.
- Leave detail flags for identifiers/counts and the business-rules column OFF for executive
  sessions — business rules are always reachable via the reveal on each driver card.
- Value Studio is where the numbers come from — open it when a figure is challenged, not before.
- "How long?" is answered in voiceover on the canvas, and on the sheet only in Solution Proposal —
  as time to value, never as a priced timeline.
- A greyed band in the Summary is PROPOSED, not counted — Promote is the only way a euro enters
  the headline, and you narrate that click.
- The internal workbench is never shown live — in a client room the only thing it produces is the
  finished proposal document, presented full-screen.
- Author field defaults to a placeholder — complete it before saving.

## What happens after the room — the handoff, briefly

The canvas is not delivery — it defines the opportunity and hands off a working file. Three more
steps carry that file to a finished, valued blueprint. Know them: they are what "what happens
next?" is asking about.

- **DEFINE — [[gls-bob-canvas|the canvas]].** The session you just ran. It ends with the exported
  working file, the `bob-client-project-spec`. **This is a one-way door:** after handover, the
  scope closes by default.
- **GENERATE — [[gls-dq-studio]].** The working file is customised there: rules converted from the
  repositories, tuned to the client's real objects, and the engagement kit (extraction lists,
  prep, profiling scripts, evidence queries, unit tests, deploy scripts) generated from it. See
  [[prc-derive-a-dq-rule|Derive a DQ Rule (single rule)]] for the rule side of that conversion.
  This is the **only** iteration loop in the whole chain, and it is bounded: findings →
  clarification → rule adjustment → re-execution, without a change-request cycle.
- **Execution — on the engagement environment.** The generated scripts run on the engagement's own
  managed environment. **Our applications never touch the client's live systems** — they ingest
  result sheets only. This is a standing fact, not a caveat: say it plainly if asked.
- **ELEVATE — [[gls-vector-studio]].** The evaluation sheet elevates into the finished, valued,
  prioritised, sequenced blueprint — down to sell, up to prove, completed. Vector-studio never
  authors SQL; weak evidence goes back to the Studio to regenerate, not fixed locally.

See [[con-value-chain]] for the chain these four steps run along.

## Verification

- You can state the inversion in your own words in under a minute, unprompted.
- You know which play you're in before the file opens, and can name what must never appear on
  screen for it.
- You can walk one outcome all the way down to a business rule using only the altitude-appropriate
  language at each level.
- You know, without checking, when to open Value Studio and when to open Rule Explorer.

## Common pitfalls

- Explaining the tool before saying the inversion out loud — Introduction's framing does the work;
  don't undercut it with a feature tour.
- Opening Value Studio before a number has actually been challenged — it's the strongest tool in
  the session; spending it early wastes the theatre.
- Showing the internal workbench tabs on a client's screen in any play — the only client-visible
  artefact from that tier is a finished document.
- Forgetting to complete the author field, or closing the tab without saving the working file —
  nothing is stored in the page.
